Plan your bills

Total listed bills $0.00One payment per bill entry; excludes loans

Choose whether to pay a bill on its due date or reserve money from one or two earlier paychecks. A $500 car payment can be allocated as $250 from each of two paychecks. Due dates remain visible for reference.

The app selects the last one or two payments from this income source on or before each bill due date. This is a planned allocation, not an automatic payment.

Build your savings

Savings contributions are planned on every income date, not actual transfers. If essential expenses exceed income, prioritize necessities.

Manage your loans

Track your remaining balance, annual interest rate, monthly payment and remaining term. Each scheduled monthly payment automatically appears on the calendar and in upcoming bills. This does not make a payment or modify your lender agreement.

The calendar schedules the entered monthly payment through the loan end date. Interest is recorded for reference; the app schedules the entered payment on each monthly due date through the end date, but does not calculate a payoff balance. Don't enter the same loan payment again in Bills.

Payday loans & cash advances

Enter the amount currently owed and the next repayment date. These repayments appear in the calendar with bills. You can record a repayment below to reduce the tracked balance. The reduction scenario changes only future hypothetical borrowing, not the current obligation.

Recording a payment updates your tracked balance, not the lender account. Check your lender statement for the actual balance, fees, and next due date.

Payday loans & payoff planning

Manage payday advances here too. Record actual repayments to update the balance you owe, and optionally plan reductions in future new borrowing. Paying an existing balance and borrowing less next time are different actions. Hypothetical later-cycle amounts are not lender-approved payment plans.

This is a scenario tool, not a promise of lender extensions or future advances. Reducing new borrowing requires enough cash to cover the gap. If your cash flow is negative, use Get Help.

Get help

Ask lenders about available repayment arrangements before a scheduled withdrawal. You can also seek assistance through 211, NFCC credit counseling, or the CFPB complaint portal.
