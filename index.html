Your borrowing exit plan

Use the recurring amount you typically reborrow after each payday. The projection assumes the previous advance is fully repaid each period and that the new advance is smaller by the selected amount.

This is a cash-flow illustration, not a repayment agreement or a guarantee. Actual repayment dates, fees, multiple advances, and bill timing may change feasibility. If the available cushion is smaller than the proposed reduction, don't follow the projection without adjusting expenses, income, or repayment arrangements.

When the math doesn't work

Some shortfalls can't be solved by cutting new borrowing alone. Prioritize food, housing, utilities, transportation to work, and other essential obligations.

Ask about repayment flexibility

Contact your advance provider or lender before the payment date. Ask what extensions, hardship programs, or repayment arrangements are available, what they cost, and whether autopay can be changed. Do not assume any provider must offer an extension.
